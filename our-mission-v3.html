Our Mission

01 — CONTEXT

A field at an inflection point.

Awareness of advanced AI — and of its safety risks — has risen sharply across the Japanese public and policy world. But national attention has concentrated almost entirely on integration: putting AI to beneficial use, rather than building the capacity to make it safe.

The result is a landscape with real momentum but a thin safety core. The people, institutions, and resources exist — they are simply scattered, and rarely pointed at frontier safety work.

  • GovernmentJapan's AI Safety Institute focuses on benchmarks and evaluations, allocating much of the work to research-consulting firms — strong on real-world business conditions, still building depth in safety research.
  • IndustryCompanies and startups are shipping AI products with an immediate need for technical, legal, and security measures — but little in-house safety context to draw on.
  • AcademiaResearch centers on AI for science. Awareness of frontline AI safety research remains low, with few channels into the international field.

02 — THE GAP

A world-class talent base — almost none of it aimed at AI safety.

Japan produces a deep, world-class engineering and research base. Yet global capacity in technical AI safety and governance remains critically limited, and virtually none of this top-percentile talent is funneled toward it.

The talent flows almost entirely into traditional roles — not for lack of ability, but because the context and infrastructure to apply those skills to AI safety simply do not yet exist locally. Where the field does exist, it is fragmented: capable people and organizations working in isolation, unaware of one another.

No coordination

Government, industry, and academia rarely meet across the technical–policy divide.

No home

No shared institution exists to turn latent talent into sustained safety contribution.

THEORY OF CHANGE

From fragmentation to a coherent community.

Today Japan's AI safety landscape is fragmented — talented people, organizations, and resources exist, but they are scattered and disconnected. Our work is to weave them into a coherent community: one where people can learn from each other, communicate across the technical and policy divide, and collaborate on problems too large for any individual or organization alone. Each seminar, conference, and program is a thread in that fabric.

03 — WHAT WE DO

Two pillars: education and connection.

Since 2023 we have run a sustained, technically serious seminar series out of central Tokyo — over one hundred sessions, each built around a single paper, report, or policy question. Around that core, our work falls into two pillars.

Pillar I

Education & training

Help researchers, engineers, and policy professionals build the skills to contribute to AI safety — from foundational courses to hands-on training and mentored projects.

  • Monthly seminar series — paper-by-paper study sessions spanning interpretability, alignment, capabilities forecasting, and AI governance.
  • TARA Tokyo — the Technical Alignment Research Accelerator, bringing structured, hands-on safety training to Tokyo.
  • Foundational courses — facilitating Japanese-language BlueDot Impact courses and delivering the Masterclass executive seminar series.
Pillar II

Connect

Weaving researchers, institutions, and decision-makers into one ecosystem — across the technical–policy divide, and across borders.

  • International conferences — co-organized TAIS 2024 and TAIS 2025 with Noeon Research, connecting Japan-based researchers with leading groups abroad.
  • Government & policy — engaging policymakers and the national network of AI alignment researchers.
  • Universities — helping build AI safety coursework, curricula, and research capacity at Japanese institutions.
  • Embassies & diplomacy — co-organizing the 2026 International AI Safety Report Briefing and Panel Discussion with British Embassy Tokyo and the UK AI Security Institute.

04 — WHERE WE'RE GOING

Scaling up, connecting the pieces.

After operating as an all-volunteer organization since January 2023, our next step is to build a professionally staffed organization. Over a proposed 12-month funding period, we plan to hire our first full-time community lead, supported by part-time operations and communications capacity, and expand our learning and partnership programs.

LEARNING

Scale practical training

Continue monthly paper-by-paper seminars and expand TARA and BlueDot Impact programs, alongside a new program focused on AI cybersecurity.

PROJECTS

Turn learners into contributors

Run a four-month incubator for around 20 fellows in project teams, supported by around eight mentors across technical safety, governance and policy, and communications. Weekly sessions and a speaker series lead to a final showcase.

COMMUNITY

Create more ways to connect

Launch monthly evening meetups for around 50 attendees, with curated talks and structured networking. Build on TAIS 2024 and TAIS 2025 with a planned one-day conference bringing together around 100 researchers, policymakers, lawyers, and engineers.

PARTNERSHIPS

Connect policy and practice

Develop an embassy luncheon series to brief policymakers and ambassadors on AI safety progress, while deepening partnerships across universities, government, embassies, and industry.
