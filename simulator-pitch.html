The central idea

Progress in AI needs more than individual talent. It needs places where that talent can find each other—and get to work.

01 / The concept

Give important work
a place to happen.

When people work in isolation, collaboration takes effort and organising events means starting from scratch. Simulator’s proposed hubs bring everyday work and community infrastructure under one roof.

▤

Space to focus

A welcoming workspace for individuals and teams, with meeting rooms, thoughtful equipment, and operational support that frees people to focus on their work.

◎

Reasons to connect

Shared meals, everyday conversations, and a community of peers. Make it easier to exchange ideas, meet collaborators, and feel part of a common endeavour.

↗

Room to grow

A reliable home for talks, workshops, reading groups, and fellowships—helping newcomers find a path into the field and organisers bring people together.

02 / How it creates value

A workspace that helps
a field grow.

The idea is simple: lower the friction of working together, then create more opportunities for useful work to emerge. This is the intended pathway to impact.

01 — GATHER

Bring people in

Give researchers, teams, newcomers, and visitors a shared destination.

02 — CONNECT

Make exchange routine

Turn occasional introductions into conversations, relationships, and shared learning.

03 — ENABLE

Help work take shape

Support collaborations, research projects, and programmes with practical infrastructure.

04 — STRENGTHEN

Grow the community

Help more people enter, contribute to, and stay in these fields.

03 / The starting point

Start in Tokyo.
Build for connection.

The proposed first hub would give Tokyo’s AI safety and digital minds communities a shared home—and a meeting point with the wider world.

Connect a dispersed community

Bring people across organisations and disciplines into regular contact, making collaboration easier to begin and sustain.

02

Bring related questions together

AI safety asks how to make advanced AI safe and beneficial. Digital minds work explores questions about AI consciousness, welfare, and moral status. A shared hub creates space for exchange across these fields.

03

Create a bridge in both directions

Help local talent connect with international researchers, and give visiting professionals a natural place to work, share ideas, and meet the community.

05 / What success would mean

Measure the work
it makes possible.

A busy room is only the beginning. The ambition is to understand whether the hub helps people work better and contributes to a stronger field.

Better working days

Less time spent on logistics; more time and support for focused work.

Useful collaborations

Relationships that lead to shared projects and research.

New paths into the field

Events and fellowships that help people learn and contribute.

A lasting community

People who stay engaged, build teams, and support one another.

The longer-term vision

Start with a hub.
Grow a network of possibility.

Learn from Tokyo, refine a repeatable approach, and help launch hubs in other cities where demand and local leadership make them valuable.
